## 2. Granular Flood Meteorological Synopses

### [2004] Catastrophic Multi-Basin Flood
- **Period & Inundation:** July–August 2004 | 38% to 40% of country submerged (49 districts, ~36.0 M people affected).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Concurrently intense Brahmaputra and Ganges monsoonal flood waves combined with sustained localized rainfall.
- **Source Authority:** FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2004; World Bank Disaster Management Studies

### [2007] Prolonged Double-Peak Monsoon Flood
- **Period & Inundation:** July–September 2007 | 32% to 42% of country submerged (40 districts, ~13.7 M people affected).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Two successive peak flood waves in late July and early September driven by extreme Himalayan transboundary inflow.
- **Source Authority:** FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2007; BUET-IWFM Technical Documentation

### [2017] Devastating Pre-Monsoon Flash Flood + August Monsoon Flood
- **Period & Inundation:** April (Haor) & August 2017 | >30% national; >70% Haor basins of country submerged (31 districts, ~6.5 M people affected).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Extreme April rainfall in Meghalaya (>1,000 mm in 10 days at Cherrapunji) submerging standing Boro crops in Sylhet haors.
- **Source Authority:** FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2017; Ministry of Disaster Management and Relief

### [2020] Historical Long-Duration Flood (~40+ days)
- **Period & Inundation:** June–August 2020 | 30% to 40% of country submerged (39 districts, ~5.0 M people affected).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Record-high seasonal monsoon precipitation across the upstream Brahmaputra basin (longest duration since 1998).
- **Source Authority:** FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2020; UN Bangladesh Humanitarian Advisory

### [2022] Centennial Record Flash Flood (Meghna/Surma-Kushiyara Basin)
- **Period & Inundation:** May–June 2022 | Extreme Localized (94% Sunamganj, 84% Sylhet) of country submerged (18 districts, ~7.2 M people affected).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Unprecedented 122-year high extreme rainfall in Meghalaya/Cherrapunji (>972 mm in 24h) causing instant flash inundation.
- **Source Authority:** FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2022; Bangladesh Meteorological Department

## 3. Scientific Synthesis: Why Floods Fail to Arrest Storage Depletion

1. **Surface Runoff vs. Subsurface Infiltration:**
   - In catastrophic flood years like **2004** (monsoon rainfall: 2,021 mm, +20.0% above 24-year mean) and **2007** (1,996 mm, +18.5% above mean), seasonal storage rebounds reached 56.7 cm and 64.8 cm respectively.
   - However, the recharge efficiency ratio remained bounded between 28% and 33%, demonstrating that deluge events exceed the soil infiltration capacity, resulting in overland ponding, drainage congestion, and estuarine discharge into the Bay of Bengal rather than unbounded subsurface retention.

2. **Transient Crests vs. Multi-Year Depletion:**
   - While catastrophic floods produce short-lived positive storage crests (+41.0 cm in 2007 and +33.1 cm in 2004), these anomalies dissipate within 3–4 months as monsoon waters drain into the delta.
   - By the subsequent pre-monsoon dry season (March–May), intensive agricultural abstraction for the dry-season Boro crop offsets the seasonal flood surplus, returning total water storage to its secular depletion trajectory.
