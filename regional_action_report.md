# Bengal Hydro Detective: Regional Stakeholder Summary

This guide translates the 2002–2026 satellite observations into localized context and literature-documented options for farmers, policymakers, and civic leaders across Bangladesh's five macro-regions.

---

## How to read this report

> **Methodological and Detection Limits:**  
> Between-region differences in total water storage were tested with a calibrated paired method. NW declines about 1.2 cm/yr faster than SW and SE; NE and Central decline about 0.5 cm/yr faster than SW and SE; SW and SE are indistinguishable; no rainfall pair differs. Exact p-values are not reported because the placebo check shows they are overconfident (see PROJECT_STATUS.md section 10). Satellite gravimetry (CSR GRACE/GRACE-FO RL0603 Mascons, ~300 km resolution) resolves regional-scale total water storage anomalies; it is not a measurement of local aquifer or pumping differences. Total water storage integrates groundwater, soil moisture, surface water, and biomass. Response ratio is defined as seasonal storage rebound divided by monsoon rainfall.

Every region below presents three distinct perspectives:
1. **What the satellite data shows:** Empirical observations from GRACE/GRACE-FO gravimetry (CSR RL0603 Mascons), GPM IMERG V07B precipitation, GLDAS land surface models, and MODIS/GPW exposure data.
2. **Context from the literature (not tested here):** Agronomic, hydrogeological, and historical background from published literature.
3. **Options others have proposed:** Documented institutional, farm-level, and civil-society initiatives from agricultural research bodies and development partners.

## 1. Northwest Region (Rajshahi & Rangpur)

### What the satellite data shows
- **Total water storage trend:** -1.25 cm/year (95% CI: [-1.50, -1.02] cm/year; -0.39 km³/year; FDR q < 0.0001; Verdict: Significant depletion).
- **Three-way transition range:** Across single-line, lead GRACE-era, and step-adjusted modeling, the depletion rate spans -1.81 to -1.25 cm/year.
- **Precipitation trend:** -1.9 mm/year (95% CI: [-11.0, +4.5] mm/year; FDR q = 0.8968; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Land and population exposure:** 38.8 million residents (22.9% of national total); 74.2% classified as cropland (MODIS MCD12Q1); 82.7% total agricultural land.
- **Seasonal dynamics:** Mean response ratio is 39.6% (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope +0.27%/year, p = 0.378, q = 0.516). Mean annual dry-season drawdown is 55.2 cm (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope +0.01 cm/year, p = 1.000, q = 1.000).
- **Storage partitioning:** Simulated unsaturated zone soil moisture (0–2 m, NASA GLDAS Noah and VIC models) exhibits a trend of -0.12 to -0.10 cm/year. Soil moisture loss represents a minor part (~8% to 10%) of total water storage decline, while the unconfined/deep storage residual accounts for -1.12 to -1.11 cm/year.

### Context from the literature (not tested here)
- Intensive dry-season Boro rice cultivation expanded rapidly following rural electrification and deep tubewell installation from the 1980s onward [source needed].
- Clay-rich topsoils across the Pleistocene Barind Tract limit rapid percolation, concentrating recharge along preferential flow pathways and depressions [source needed].
- Persistent seasonal pumping depressurizes subsurface storage prior to monsoon onset, creating a heavy reliance on mechanical lift [source needed].

### Options others have proposed
- **Irrigation management:** BRRI reports that Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD) reduces field irrigation water application by 15% to 30% without penalizing grain yield [1].
- **Metering infrastructure:** BMDA reports that digital prepaid smart-card meters installed on deep tubewells automate volumetric billing and eliminate unmonitored pumping [2].
- **Crop diversification:** Agricultural research institutes have proposed shifting portions of high-water-demand Boro acreage to low-water winter pulses, oilseeds, or wheat [source needed].

## 2. Northeast Haor Basin (Sylhet & Mymensingh)

### What the satellite data shows
- **Total water storage trend:** -0.56 cm/year (95% CI: [-0.70, -0.42] cm/year; -0.12 km³/year; FDR q < 0.0001; Verdict: Significant depletion).
- **Precipitation trend:** -0.6 mm/year (95% CI: [-15.4, +16.1] mm/year; FDR q = 0.9342; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Land and population exposure:** 24.1 million residents (14.2% of national total); 58.6% classified as cropland; 69.8% total agricultural land.
- **Seasonal dynamics:** Mean annual dry-season drawdown is 58.5 cm (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope +0.09 cm/year, p = 0.817, q = 1.000). Mean response ratio is 30.1% (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope +0.20%/year, p = 0.215, q = 0.429).
- **Cross-region comparison:** Calibrated paired testing indicates NE declines about 0.5 cm/year faster than SW and SE, but does not differ significantly from Central.

### Context from the literature (not tested here)
- The depression geography of the haor basin creates seasonal transitions between vast inland water bodies during the monsoon and intensively farmed paddy fields in winter [source needed].
- Early pre-monsoon flash floods originating in upstream Meghalaya and Assam catchments present recurrent risks to ripening winter rice prior to harvest [source needed].

### Options others have proposed
- **Hydraulic protection:** BWDB reports that submersible earthen embankments are designed to stay below peak monsoon flood stages while delaying early pre-monsoon flash floods until Boro harvest [3].
- **Varietal selection:** BRRI has evaluated early-maturing rice varieties designed to mature prior to early flash flood windows [source needed].
- **Floating agriculture:** FAO designated Bangladesh's floating garden agricultural practices (*dhap*) as a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System (GIAHS), documenting water-hyacinth raft cultivation during seasonal inundation [4].

## 3. Central Region (Dhaka Division)

### What the satellite data shows
- **Total water storage trend:** -0.47 cm/year (95% CI: [-0.62, -0.34] cm/year; -0.08 km³/year; FDR q < 0.0001; Verdict: Significant depletion).
- **Precipitation trend:** +0.9 mm/year (95% CI: [-9.7, +12.7] mm/year; FDR q = 0.9342; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Land and population exposure:** 45.6 million residents (26.9% of national total; highest density); 56.4% classified as cropland; 66.2% total agricultural land.
- **Seasonal dynamics:** Mean response ratio is 42.2% (highest among regions; suggestive slope +0.39%/year, p = 0.0369, q = 0.1106 in the main run). Mean annual dry-season drawdown is 58.2 cm (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope -0.01 cm/year, p = 0.944, q = 1.000).
- **Storage partitioning:** Simulated soil moisture trends are negligible (-0.08 to -0.04 cm/year), leaving deep residual loss as the primary component of total storage reduction.

### Context from the literature (not tested here)
- Dense municipal populations and concentrated manufacturing sectors abstract large volumes of groundwater from the Dupi Tila sand formation [source needed].
- Surface paving and impervious infrastructure across the Dhaka metropolitan area restrict localized diffuse surface infiltration [source needed].

### Options others have proposed
- **Building standards:** The Ministry of Housing and Public Works incorporates provisions for rainwater harvesting and recharge systems into the Bangladesh National Building Code (BNBC) [5].
- **Industrial water recycling:** Water resource agencies have recommended zero-liquid-discharge systems and wastewater recycling for peri-urban industrial belts [source needed].
- **Urban green space:** Municipal planning frameworks propose conserving wetlands and retention ponds to mitigate urban runoff [source needed].

## 4. Southwest Coastal Delta (Khulna & Barishal)

### What the satellite data shows
- **Total water storage trend:** -0.05 cm/year (95% CI: [-0.20, +0.10] cm/year; -0.01 km³/year; FDR q = 0.8968; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Precipitation trend:** +2.2 mm/year (95% CI: [-7.3, +14.6] mm/year; FDR q = 0.8968; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Land and population exposure:** 27.1 million residents (16.0% of national total); 48.7% classified as cropland; 56.1% total agricultural land; encompasses the Sundarbans mangrove forest.
- **Seasonal dynamics:** Mean response ratio is 33.0% (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope +0.14%/year, p = 0.430, q = 0.516). Mean annual dry-season drawdown is 48.7 cm (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope -0.10 cm/year, p = 0.501, q = 1.000).
- **Cross-region comparison:** Calibrated paired testing shows no detectable difference between SW and SE total water storage trends.

### Context from the literature (not tested here)
- Dry-season estuarine salinity intrusion alters freshwater availability in surface rivers and shallow subsurface zones, despite bulk mass conservation in satellite measurements [source needed].
- Coastal polders regulate tidal water exchange but require dynamic gate operation to manage seasonal waterlogging and soil salinity [source needed].

### Options others have proposed
- **Saline-tolerant cropping:** BRRI reports that salt-tolerant rice variety BRRI dhan67 tolerates seedling-stage water electrical conductivity up to 12 dS/m and yields 3.0 to 6.1 t/ha in coastal environments [6].
- **Polder water control:** Coastal engineers propose synchronized sluice gate operation to retain late-monsoon freshwater within polder canals before estuarine salinity elevates [source needed].
- **Drinking water access:** Community initiatives utilize Pond Sand Filters (PSF) and household rainwater harvesting tanks to provide potable water in saline zones [source needed].

## 5. Southeast Hill Tracts (Chattogram & CHT)

### What the satellite data shows
- **Total water storage trend:** -0.05 cm/year (95% CI: [-0.17, +0.06] cm/year; -0.01 km³/year; FDR q = 0.8968; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Precipitation trend:** +1.8 mm/year (95% CI: [-9.4, +13.8] mm/year; FDR q = 0.9342; Verdict: No detectable trend).
- **Land and population exposure:** 34.2 million residents (20.1% of national total); 32.1% classified as cropland (lowest cropland share); 46.7% total agricultural land.
- **Seasonal dynamics:** Mean response ratio is 26.0% (lowest among regions; Verdict: No detectable trend; slope +0.06%/year, p = 0.612, q = 0.612). Mean annual dry-season drawdown is 51.2 cm (Verdict: No detectable trend; slope -0.05 cm/year, p = 0.700, q = 1.000).
- **Cross-region comparison:** Calibrated paired testing finds total water storage trends in SE indistinguishable from SW.

### Context from the literature (not tested here)
- Steep topography and Tertiary sandstone ridges produce high surface runoff fractions during monsoon events, resulting in lower seasonal storage rebound ratios [source needed].
- Remote upland communities depend on seasonal streams and perennial hill springs (*jharna*) for household water supplies [source needed].

### Options others have proposed
- **Watershed conservation:** ICIMOD and UNDP report that indigenous Village Common Forests (VCFs) protect headwater micro-watersheds feeding perennial hill springs [7].
- **Soil conservation farming:** ICIMOD has documented Sloping Agricultural Land Technology (SALT) contour hedgerows to diffuse runoff and build bio-terraces on sloping hillsides [8].
- **Agroforestry practices:** Regional development bodies promote perennial fruit orchards on uplands to minimize soil erosion [source needed].

## What this report does not claim

1. **No local-scale depth-to-water claims:** Satellite gravimetry integrates mass anomalies across footprints of approximately 300 km. It cannot measure individual tubewell pumping water levels, localized water tables, or village-level pumping lift.
2. **No causal attribution:** Gravimetric trends quantify total mass change. Observations showing storage decline alongside absent precipitation trends are consistent with, but do not definitively prove, specific human pumping or climate mechanisms.
3. **No water quality or salinity measurements:** Gravimetric sensors measure bulk mass. They cannot detect chemical concentrations, arsenic presence, or salinity levels. Coastal salinity challenges exist independently of bulk water storage conservation.
4. **No blanket policy mandate:** The options cited above represent documented literature proposals and institutional research; their suitability varies with micro-topography, hydrogeology, and community preferences.
