3. Subsurface Storage Partitioning & Sensor Transition Integrity

Evaluating soil moisture contributions, the 2017–2018 inter-satellite gap, and precipitation sensor calibration.

Physical Partition & Sensor Calibration

3A. Multi-Model Soil Moisture Partitioning (NASA GLDAS)

Open-loop land surface models (Noah 2.1 & VIC 2.1) simulate topsoil moisture (top 2 meters) purely from atmospheric forcing (rainfall, solar radiation, temperature, wind). In the Northwest full record, topsoil drying accounts for only [-0.12, -0.10] cm/yr, leaving a dominant subsurface deficit of [-1.12, -1.11] cm/yr strictly labeled: "groundwater + surface water + model error residual".

Analytical Window Region TWS Slope (cm/yr) Independent Soil Moisture Range Independent Residual Range CLSM Soil Moisture Slope CLSM Residual Slope
Methodological Quarantine Notice: Catchment Land Surface Model (CLSM 2.2) assimilates GRACE satellite gravity observations via an Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF). Because CLSM directly ingests GRACE data to adjust its soil moisture fields, using CLSM to explain GRACE constitutes circular reasoning. CLSM attributes -0.94 cm/yr to soil moisture, artificially compressing the residual to -0.33 cm/yr. CLSM is quarantined from the independent physical partition.

3B. Inter-Satellite Transition Gap Sensitivity (2017–2018) Intercalibration Audit

Between GRACE decommissioning (2017-06) and GRACE-FO startup (2018-06), an upward step offset (+9.15 cm in NW, p = 0.028) of unknown origin is detected in the raw gravimetric anomaly series. Because Monte Carlo red-noise simulations show that an unadjusted step is falsely flagged as a trend in 93.8% to 100% of simulations under standard linear models, multi-satellite linear trends across unbridged gaps must always be reported alongside step-adjusted and buffer-excluded bounds. The Northwest rate is bounded between -1.81 and -1.25 cm/year (lead benchmark: -1.56 cm/year).

3C. Rainfall Sensor Calibration: Reanalysis Artifact vs. Satellite Ground Truth

NASA POWER Reanalysis Step Artifact

Earlier project drafts noted surging rainfall (+29 to +86 mm/yr). Systematic homogeneity audits identified this as a non-climatic data artifact in NASA POWER (MERRA-2 reanalysis), which underwent an abrupt upward jump of +45% to +84% between December 2014 and December 2015 (SNHT T = 31.94, p < 0.0001). In the Northwest, annual totals shifted artificially from ~1,580 mm to ~2,330 mm.

Unbiased Ground Truth with GPM IMERG V07B

Replacing reanalysis with dedicated radar/microwave satellite observations (GPM IMERG V07B Final Run) eliminated the artificial jump. Over 24 years, national rainfall trend is +2.04 mm/year (p = 0.64, FDR q = 0.9342, no detectable trend). Northwest rainfall is -1.91 mm/year (p = 0.54, no detectable trend). Multi-year IMERG national rainfall averages 2,366 mm/year.

5. Scientific Limitations & Master Data Appendix

Upholding strict scientific integrity by documenting observational boundaries and providing the complete 336-test audit database.

Audit Standards
Observational Pattern What It Cannot Prove Resolution Pathway
1. Regional Storage Depletion Coarse gravimetric resolution (~300 km) cannot resolve district, upazila, or municipal drawdown. Satellite radar interferometry (InSAR) ground deformation and sub-basin subsidence modeling.
2. Residual Subsurface Loss Satellites measure total column mass; we cannot separate deep groundwater from surface water bodies without ground well data. Direct integration of BWDB nationwide in-situ piezometer well hydrographs.
3. Agricultural Pumping Link We do not possess direct metered pumping logs across the Barind agricultural tract. Sub-district agricultural power consumption records and deep tubewell discharge registries from the BMDA.
4. Satellite Transition Gap The 11-month transition (2017–2018) between GRACE and GRACE-FO introduces an upward offset (+9.15 cm, p=0.028) of unknown origin. Continuous multi-satellite airborne gravimetry bridging or hydrodynamic data assimilation.
5. Surrogate Placebo Sensitivity On a real-residual surrogate placebo, NW previously showed an elevated False Positive Rate of 17.6% (82.5% CI coverage) under ESS-MK, whereas under independent AR(1) Monte Carlo null test, Calibrated ESS-MK passes nominal standards (4.10% FPR, 95.90% coverage). Both tests are reported to document sensitivity to empirical noise structure; longer continuous records will narrow this uncertainty.
6. Precipitation Latency GPM IMERG Final Run V07B currently extends through September 2025 due to NASA GES DISC latency, truncating the final 10 months. Updating the processing stream when NASA releases late-2025 and 2026 Final Run granules.
7. Descriptive Nature of Divisions Administrative divisions cannot be treated as statistically independent hydrological catchments. Divisions are strictly labeled descriptive; hypothesis tests are evaluated at the 5-region and National scale.
8. Untested Flood Mechanism The hypothesis that topsoil saturation limits deep infiltration during catastrophic floods remains unverified. Direct borehole piezometer infiltration measurements during monsoon flood waves.
