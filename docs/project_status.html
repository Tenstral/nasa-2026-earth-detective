1. What Is This Project and What Question Does It Answer? Foundational Scope

Every summer, Bangladesh receives substantial monsoon rainfall, nourishing river networks and occasionally causing major seasonal flooding. Yet farmers and local water managers in the northern agricultural heartland report that tubewells run dry during the pre-monsoon dry season and water tables have declined over recent decades.

"Is Bangladesh losing total water storage over the long term, how do regional storage trajectories compare to satellite precipitation trends, and does unsaturated soil moisture account for observed storage changes?"

To evaluate these dynamics empirically, we developed a reproducible scientific detection pipeline that audits 24 continuous years of NASA satellite observations (April 2002 to July 2026). The analysis synthesizes four authoritative pillars:

Pillar 1: Gravimetry
CSR GRACE / GRACE-FO
RL06.3 Mascons tracking vertically integrated total water storage mass (April 2002 – July 2026).
Pillar 2: Precipitation
GPM IMERG V07B Final
Dual-frequency radar and passive microwave calibrated satellite precipitation constellation.
Pillar 3: Soil Moisture
GLDAS Land Surface
Open-loop physical simulations (Noah 2.1 & VIC 2.1) tracking moisture in the top 2 meters of soil.
Pillar 4: Exposure
SEDAC & MODIS
Harmonized Census 2022 population grids (GPWv4) and 500m cropland coverage (MCD12Q1).

Spatial Scale & Test Hierarchy

  • Regional vs. Divisional Analysis: Satellite gravimetry operates at an effective spatial resolution of approximately 300 km. Consequently, individual administrative divisions cannot be treated as statistically independent hydrological catchments. We aggregate data into 5 hydrologically cohesive macro-regions (Northwest, Northeast, Central, Southwest, Southeast) and a National composite. Administrative division series are provided strictly for descriptive and local administrative context.
  • The 336 Statistical Tests: The master results file (results_all_windows.csv) audits exactly 336 individual tests: 4 analytical windows × 14 spatial units (6 macro-regions + 8 administrative divisions) × 2 variables (TWS, IMERG precipitation) × 3 seasonal slices (annual, dry season, monsoon season) = 4 × 14 × 2 × 3 = 336 tests. All formal hypothesis testing and False Discovery Rate (FDR) screening are evaluated at the 5-region and National scale.

B. The 3-Way Northwest Slope Transparency

Between the original GRACE mission (decommissioned June 2017) and GRACE Follow-On (operational June 2018), an 11-month observation gap occurred. An upward step offset (+9.15 cm in NW, p = 0.028) of unknown origin is detected across the gap in raw gravimetric anomaly series. Because multi-satellite trends across unbridged gaps can be sensitive to step offsets, we report the Northwest trend across three transparent model specifications:

Method / Model Specification Northwest TWS Slope 95% Confidence Interval Methodological Description
1. Lead Evidence: GRACE-1 Era (2002–2017) -1.56 cm/yr [-1.96, -1.17] cm/yr Transition-free baseline under a single continuous satellite instrument.
2. Single Continuous Line (2002–2026) -1.25 cm/yr [-1.50, -1.02] cm/yr Standard linear trend fit across the full 24-year record under Calibrated ESS-MK.
3. Step-Adjusted Fit (2002–2026) -1.81 cm/yr — Controls for a +9.15 cm upward step offset (p = 0.028) across the 2017–2018 transition.

Takeaway: Across continuous, step-adjusted, and transition-excluded specifications, Northwest multi-satellite total water storage depletion spans an estimated range of -1.81 to -1.25 cm/year (with the single-satellite lead benchmark at -1.56 cm/year). Pure step simulations show that an unadjusted +9.15 cm step is falsely flagged as a trend in 93.8% to 100% of red-noise simulations under standard linear models, demonstrating why step-adjusted and buffer-excluded bounds are necessary.

4. The Rainfall Record: Data Artifact vs. Satellite Observation Audit & Validation

A. The NASA POWER Reanalysis Artifact

Earlier exploratory evaluations reported that Bangladesh rainfall was increasing rapidly (+29 to +86 mm/year). Systematic data audit identified this as a reanalysis data artifact:

  • Between 2014-12 and 2015-12, NASA POWER's underlying assimilation system (MERRA-2) underwent an abrupt upward step change of +45% to +84% across Bangladesh (Pettitt p < 0.0001, SNHT T = 31.94).
  • In the Northwest, annual totals jumped from ~1,580 mm/year prior to 2015 to ~2,330 mm/year after 2018.
  • This non-climatic step shift induced spurious positive linear trends. The exact cause in the reanalysis model assimilation stream remains unverified.

B. Satellite Precipitation with GPM IMERG: No Detectable Rainfall Trend

Replacing atmospheric reanalysis with NASA's radar and microwave satellite precipitation constellation (GPM IMERG V07B Final Run) eliminated the artificial jump:

  • National IMERG Trend: +2.04 mm/year (95% CI: [-7.70, +12.45] mm/yr, p = 0.64, FDR q = 0.9342) — showing no detectable trend within natural multi-year variability.
  • Northwest IMERG Trend: -1.91 mm/year (95% CI: [-11.04, +4.48] mm/yr, p = 0.54, FDR q = 0.8968) — showing no detectable trend.
  • Long-Term Mean: Over the satellite observational period (2002-01 to 2025-09), national IMERG precipitation averages 2,366 mm/year.
  • Data Latency Notice: GPM IMERG Final data currently extends through September 2025 due to standard NASA production latency, truncating the final 10 months of the 2002–2026 gravimetry analysis window.

C. Extreme Rainfall and Flood Dynamics

  • No Detectable Trend in Extreme Indices: Daily precipitation extremes (Rx1day, Rx5day, days ≥ 50 mm, and annual total for 2003–2024; monsoon total, monsoon Rx1day, monsoon Rx5day, and monsoon days ≥ 50 mm for 2002–2025) show no detectable trend across Bangladesh.
  • Flood Response & Storage Recovery (Untested Hypothesis): Historical flood events (2004, 2007, 2017, 2020, 2022 Sylhet) correspond to large transient storage surges (e.g., storage anomalies spiked by +41 cm in 2007 and +33 cm in 2004). An untested hypothesis frequently cited in regional hydrogeology suggests topsoil saturation and floodplain drainage limit the proportion of floodwaters that recharge deeper subsurface storage. However, satellite gravimetry captures vertically integrated bulk mass and cannot measure subsurface infiltration rates directly without in-situ piezometer hydrographs.

5. Storage Partitioning: Soil Moisture vs. Unpartitioned Residual GLDAS Physical Decomposition

Satellite gravimetry measures total column water mass, integrating surface water, soil moisture, and deep groundwater. To determine whether total water storage depletion is accounted for by unsaturated soil drying, we evaluated NASA's GLDAS Land Surface Models across all four analytical windows:

Analytical Window TWS Slope Independent Soil Moisture (ΔSMS) Partitioned Residual Physical Status
Full Record (2002–2026) -1.25 cm/yr [-0.12, -0.10] cm/yr [-1.12, -1.11] cm/yr Significant Deficit
GRACE-1 Era (2002–2017) -1.56 cm/yr [-0.54, -0.28] cm/yr [-1.26, -1.05] cm/yr Lead Baseline Deficit
GRACE-FO Era (2018–2026) -1.67 cm/yr [+0.05, +0.20] cm/yr [-1.92, -1.70] cm/yr Below Detection Limit
Recent Window (2020–2026) -2.93 cm/yr [-0.37, -0.22] cm/yr [-2.59, -2.38] cm/yr Method-Dependent (q = 0.077)
Physical Interpretation: Across all windows, unsaturated root-zone soil moisture decline accounts for only a minor fraction of observed total water storage loss. Most of the decline is not accounted for by simulated soil moisture; groundwater and surface water cannot be separated here. This pattern is consistent with, not proof of, dry-season groundwater abstraction for intensive irrigated agriculture.
CLSM 2.2 Methodological Quarantine: Catchment Land Surface Model (CLSM 2.2) directly assimilates GRACE TWS observations via an Ensemble Kalman Filter (EnKF). In CLSM, simulated soil moisture declines rapidly (-0.94 cm/yr in full_record), artificially compressing the residual to -0.33 cm/yr. Because assimilating GRACE into CLSM to evaluate GRACE involves circular logic, CLSM is reported separately for context and quarantined from the independent physical budget.

6. Honest Limitations & Resolution Pathways Scientific Integrity

To preserve scientific integrity, observed patterns are subject to the following limitations:

Observational Pattern What It Cannot Prove What Would Be Needed to Resolve It
1. Regional Water Depletion Coarse gravimetric resolution (~300 km) cannot resolve district, upazila, or municipal drawdown. Satellite radar interferometry (InSAR) ground deformation and sub-basin subsidence modeling.
2. Residual Subsurface Loss Satellites measure total column mass; we cannot separate deep groundwater from surface water bodies and wetlands. Direct integration of Bangladesh Water Development Board (BWDB) nationwide in-situ piezometer well hydrographs.
3. Agricultural Extraction Link We do not possess direct metered pumping logs across the Barind Tract. Sub-district agricultural power consumption records and deep tubewell discharge registries from the Barind Multipurpose Development Authority (BMDA).
4. Inter-Satellite Gap & Offset The 11-month transition (2017–2018) between GRACE and GRACE-FO introduces an upward offset (+9.15 cm, p = 0.028) of unknown origin. Continuous multi-satellite airborne gravimetry bridging or hydrodynamic data assimilation across the transition.
5. Surrogate Placebo Sensitivity On a real-residual surrogate placebo (phase-randomized surrogates preserving autocorrelation and gap pattern), NW previously showed an elevated False Positive Rate of 17.6% (82.5% CI coverage) under ESS-MK, whereas under an independent parametric AR(1) Monte Carlo null test, Calibrated ESS-MK passes nominal standards (NW FPR: 4.10%, coverage: 95.90%). Both tests are reported to document sensitivity to empirical noise structure; longer continuous observational records will narrow this uncertainty.
6. Precipitation Record Latency GPM IMERG Final Run V07B currently extends through September 2025. Updating the processing stream when NASA GES DISC releases late-2025 and 2026 Final Run granules.

10. Pairwise Between-Region Slope Comparison (2002–2026) Paired Difference Inferences

Evaluates whether secular Theil-Sen trend slopes differ between the 5 macro-regions on common valid observation months (N = 257 for TWS; N = 247 for IMERG rainfall) using the calibrated Difference Series Effective-Sample-Size Mann-Kendall engine (run_ess_mann_kendall).

Summary of Primary Findings

  • Total Water Storage Trends Differ Across 9 of 10 Regional Pairs Under Paired Difference Testing:
    • Northwest (NW) differs significantly from all other regions: The NW decline (-1.255 cm/yr) is steeper than Northeast (Δ = -0.653 cm/yr, q < 0.0001), Central (Δ = -0.722 cm/yr, q < 0.0001), Southwest (Δ = -1.205 cm/yr, q < 0.0001), and Southeast (Δ = -1.189 cm/yr, q < 0.0001).
    • Northeast & Central differ significantly from the South: NE and Central show steeper declines than SW and SE (each Δ ≈ -0.45 to -0.54 cm/yr, all q < 0.0001).
    • Northeast vs Central: The paired difference test yields Δ = -0.072 cm/yr (95% CI: [-0.101, -0.043], p < 0.0001, FDR q < 0.0001). However, under independent comparison, their single-region 95% CIs overlap substantially ([-0.703, -0.419] and [-0.620, -0.342]). Differencing removes shared regional climate variance (ρ = 0.945), making a small differential trend detectable even though marginal trends overlap.
    • Southwest vs Southeast show no detectable difference: SW and SE trends yield Δ = +0.006 cm/yr (95% CI: [-0.033, +0.045], p = 0.7345, FDR q = 0.7731).
  • Rainfall Trends Show No Detectable Difference Across ANY Regional Pair: All 10 pairs show no detectable difference for GPM IMERG rainfall (all p ≥ 0.14, all within-family FDR q ≥ 0.28). Minimum detectable slope differences at 80% power range from 11.2 to 28.7 mm/yr; observed differences are consistent with natural random variability.

Calibration Check & Real-Residual Placebo Audit

  • Nominal Calibration Audit: Candidate A (Difference Series Calibrated ESS-MK) passed calibration across 100% of tested pairs. Across all 20 tested pairs (10 TWS + 10 rainfall), False Positive Rate ranges from 3.60% to 6.50% (mean 4.93%) and 95% CI coverage ranges from 93.50% to 96.40%, meeting all pre-specified targets.
  • Real-Residual Placebo Audit: Under Synchronized Circular Shift (preserving contemporaneous cross-correlation ρ), Central vs SW (FPR = 5.90%, coverage = 94.10%) and SW vs SE (FPR = 5.00%, coverage = 95.00%) pass nominal standards. Eight pairs fail due to empirical multi-annual excursions and carry the flag Fails real-residual placebo alongside their existing verdicts.
Methodological Limits & Scope: "Regions are adjacent and GRACE resolves a few hundred kilometres, so neighbouring regions share signal and coastal leakage is possible. A significant difference between two regions' slopes is a statement about these satellite-derived series, not a measurement of local aquifer or pumping differences. Recent windows are below the detection limit."
