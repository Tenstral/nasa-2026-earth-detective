1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE HYPOTHESIS TEST
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• The core question investigated is whether rainfall and total water storage (TWS) trend differently across the hydrologic regions of Bangladesh, and whether regional differences are statistically significant.
• We identify a profound spatial decoupling termed the 'Bengal Water Paradox':
  - Across all 5 hydrologic regions, precipitation trends exhibit no detectable trend (Hamed-Rao p > 0.50 everywhere),
    ranging between -20.50 mm/yr (-7.21 %/decade, p = 0.6969) in the SW to +11.84 mm/yr (+4.56 %/decade, p = 0.7930) in Central Bangladesh (+1.23 mm/yr or +0.53 %/decade in the NW, p = 0.9678).
  - In striking contrast, total water storage shows severe, statistically confirmed depletion in the Northwest (NW = -2.93 cm/yr [95% bootstrap CI: -4.61 to -1.13 cm/yr], equating to -0.91 km³/yr volumetric loss; Hamed-Rao p = 0.0002, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR q = 0.0021). The Northwest is the sole region meeting the rigorous two-part significance standard (passes BH-FDR q < 0.05 AND 95% CI excludes zero). This confirmed depletion is driven by both Rangpur (-3.42 cm/yr, p = 0.0002, Significant) and Rajshahi (-2.40 cm/yr, p = 0.0013, Significant).
  - Central Bangladesh (Central = -1.19 cm/yr, p = 0.0309, q = 0.1235; Dhaka Division = -1.19 cm/yr, p = 0.0309) and Mymensingh Division (-1.69 cm/yr, p = 0.0194, q = 0.0884) exhibit suggestive downward tendencies based on raw p < 0.05. However, because their FDR q-values exceed 0.05 and Central's bootstrap 95% CI includes zero [-2.43, +0.09 cm/yr], their formal verdict is classified as 'Suggestive decrease' and they do not count as statistically confirmed depletion.
  - In Southern and Eastern regions (SW and SE), as well as Northeast (NE), total water storage exhibits no detectable trend (SW = -0.12 cm/yr, p = 0.7725; SE = +0.39 cm/yr, p = 0.3106; NE = -0.99 cm/yr, p = 0.1003).
  - Nationally (area-weighted across all 8 divisions, 122,353 km²), the composite total water storage rate is -1.02 cm/yr (volume rate -1.24 km³/yr; 95% CI [-2.17, +0.26] cm/yr; p = 0.1168, q = 0.3036), which represents no detectable trend nationally, demonstrating that national aggregation masks severe localized depletion in the Northwest.

3. REGIONAL DIVERGENCE TESTS: ARE REGIONAL DIFFERENCES REAL?
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• A central question in Earth observation is establishing whether differences between geographical units represent physical realities or mere sampling noise. Using paired circular block-bootstrap hypothesis testing (9,999 resamples with synchronized resample indexing across regions):
  1. NW vs SE: Storage difference Δ = -2.12 cm/year (exact bootstrap p = 0.0002, BH-FDR q = 0.0021). The Northwest is losing water significantly faster than Southeastern Bangladesh.
  2. NW vs SW: Storage difference Δ = -1.96 cm/year (exact bootstrap p = 0.0001, BH-FDR q = 0.0017). Northwest depletion significantly exceeds the Southwest.
  3. Central vs SE: Storage difference Δ = -1.26 cm/year (exact bootstrap p = 0.0001, BH-FDR q = 0.0017).
  4. Central vs SW: Storage difference Δ = -1.10 cm/year (exact bootstrap p = 0.0001, BH-FDR q = 0.0017).
  5. NW vs Central: Storage difference Δ = -0.86 cm/year (p = 0.0502, not significant after FDR).
  6. NW vs NE: Storage difference Δ = -0.92 cm/year (p = 0.0682, not significant after FDR).
• In sharp contrast, while pairwise difference tests for precipitation showed nominal raw variations between some pairs, zero precipitation differences survive Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control (all FDR q >= 0.05 across all pairs). This confirms that meteorologically, rainfall trends show no detectable regional divergence across the nation, whereas total water storage trends are profoundly divergent.

5. SEASONAL ASYMMETRY & EXPLORATORY STORAGE SWING PARTITIONING
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Seasonal budget analysis reveals substantial seasonal storage dynamics in the Northwest:
  - Dry Season (Dec–Mar): NW experiences marked water drawdown (mean anomaly: -33.50 cm), where dry-season rainfall averages 23.3 mm/month (2.89 km³ total volume).
  - Monsoon Season (Jun–Sep): NW receives 392.4 mm/month of rain (48.63 km³ total volume), and total water storage crests at -1.44 cm mean anomaly.
  - Exploratory Storage Swing Partitioning: In the NW, the seasonal storage swing is 9.94 km³ (32.07 cm), which corresponds to 20.4% of the monsoon rainfall volume influx (48.63 km³). Exploratory seasonal swing analysis indicates that approximately ~20% of monsoon rainfall manifests as seasonal storage swing, with the remainder partitioned into unobserved hydrological components including surface runoff, evapotranspiration, and river drainage.
  - Descriptive Year-by-Year Recharge (N = 6 complete monsoon seasons, 2020–2025): Reported descriptively with no trend test or change-point claimed due to the short sample size.

7. PLAUSIBLE PHYSICAL MECHANISMS (CONSISTENT WITH OBSERVED PATTERNS)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Agricultural Irrigation (Barind Tract): The Northwest is Bangladesh's primary agricultural granary, dominated by Boro rice cultivation during the dry winter season. Boro requires an estimated 3,000 to 5,000 liters of water per kilogram produced. Intensive dry-season irrigation reliant on hundreds of thousands of shallow and deep tubewells is consistent with the observed rapid dry-season drawdown and declining multi-year TWS trend.
• Hydrogeological Characteristics: The Pleistocene clay residuum (Madhupur and Barind Clay formations) has low vertical hydraulic conductivity, creating semi-confined to confined conditions that impede rapid monsoon recharge to deeper aquifers. This is consistent with the observed seasonal storage swing dynamics.
• Megacity Abstraction: In Central Bangladesh, Dhaka's municipal and industrial water demand draws an estimated 3+ million m³/day, producing an extensive cone of depression in the Plio-Pleistocene Dupi Tila aquifer. This is consistent with Central's suggestive TWS decline (-1.19 cm/yr), though regional FDR control leaves this finding formally inconclusive.
• Upstream River Regulation: Regional dry-season surface inflow through transboundary rivers (Ganges/Padma, Teesta) is governed by upstream barrages. Reduced dry-season discharge limits river-aquifer recharge, consistent with prolonged storage recovery times.
