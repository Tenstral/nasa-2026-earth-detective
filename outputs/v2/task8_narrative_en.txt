1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & CORE HYPOTHESIS TEST
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
• Core research question: Do rainfall and total water storage (TWS) trend differently across Bangladesh's hydrologic regions, and are those differences statistically significant?
• Satellite data reveal a pronounced north–south spatial gradient in TWS that has no counterpart in the precipitation record — termed here the 'Bengal Water Paradox'.
• Rainfall (Primary window 2020–2026, 79 obs; GRACE-FO window 2018–2026, 96 obs reported alongside):
  All 5 regions and the National composite show no detectable trend in annual precipitation (Hamed-Rao p > 0.05 everywhere across both observational windows).
  - Northwest (NW): Primary = +1.2 mm/yr (+0.53 %/decade, p = 0.9678); 2018-window = +12.1 mm/yr (+5.19 %/decade, p = 0.5123).
  - Southwest (SW): Primary = -20.5 mm/yr (-7.21 %/decade, p = 0.6969); 2018-window = -75.7 mm/yr (-24.68 %/decade, p = 0.2602).
  - Central: Primary = +11.8 mm/yr (+4.56 %/decade, p = 0.7930); 2018-window = -8.2 mm/yr (-3.08 %/decade, p = 0.7879).
  - Northeast (NE): Primary = -1.0 mm/yr (-0.25 %/decade, p = 0.9675); 2018-window = +1.0 mm/yr (+0.24 %/decade, p = 0.9752).
  - Southeast (SE): Primary = -5.2 mm/yr (-1.27 %/decade, p = 0.9468); 2018-window = -109.8 mm/yr (-23.98 %/decade, p = 0.1814).
  - National Composite: Primary = -21.4 mm/yr (-6.88 %/decade, p = 0.5215); 2018-window = -39.6 mm/yr (-12.05 %/decade, p = 0.2428).
  All 95% bootstrap CIs are wide and include zero — the precipitation signal is inconclusive, not a confirmed stable value.
• Total Water Storage (TWS):
  The Northwest (NW) shows severe, statistically confirmed depletion (-2.93 cm/yr, N = 79 obs, 95% CI [-4.61, -1.13] cm/yr, -0.91 km³/yr; Hamed-Rao p = 0.0002, BH-FDR q = 0.0021; verdict: Significant decrease). This is the sole region meeting the rigorous two-part significance standard.
  NW constituent divisions: Rangpur -3.42 cm/yr (p = 0.0002, q = 0.0021, Significant decrease); Rajshahi -2.40 cm/yr (p = 0.0013, q = 0.0093, Significant decrease).
  Central: -1.19 cm/yr (N = 79, CI [-2.43, +0.09] cm/yr, p = 0.0309, q = 0.1235; CI includes zero → Suggestive decrease).
  Mymensingh: -1.69 cm/yr (p = 0.0194, q = 0.0884; Suggestive decrease).
  SW = -0.12 cm/yr (p = 0.7725, No detectable trend); SE = +0.39 cm/yr (p = 0.3106, No detectable trend); NE = -0.99 cm/yr (p = 0.1003, No detectable trend).
  National composite (area-weighted, 79 obs): -1.02 cm/yr (CI [-2.17, +0.26] cm/yr, p = 0.1168, q = 0.3036; No detectable trend). National aggregation obscures the localized NW signal.

7. PLAUSIBLE PHYSICAL MECHANISMS (CONSISTENT WITH — NOT CAUSED BY — OBSERVATIONS)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Note: All numeric claims in this section must be verified against cited sources. Uncited quantities are deleted; only qualitative consistency is stated.
• Agricultural abstraction (Barind Tract / Rajshahi-Rangpur): Intensive Boro rice cultivation in the dry season relies on shallow and deep tubewell irrigation. The spatial overlap of the confirmed TWS decline with the Boro cultivation belt is consistent with demand-side extraction exceeding natural recharge — a pattern previously documented using GRACE data by Shamsudduha et al. (2009, Hydrogeology Journal) and Biswas (2011). Specific water-use coefficients are not reproduced here as they require independent verification against the cited literature.
• Hydrogeological setting: The Barind Clay (Pleistocene residual clay) has low vertical hydraulic conductivity, consistent with slow monsoon recharge of deeper aquifers. Supporting characterisation: BADC/BWDB monitoring reports (cited in national water plans).
• Dhaka urban demand: Dhaka Metropolitan area abstracts from the Dupi Tila aquifer; reported declining groundwater levels are consistent with a suggestive TWS decline in Central Bangladesh. Specific extraction volumes require verification from DWASA annual reports.
• Upstream river regulation: Transboundary river flows (Ganges/Padma, Teesta) are regulated by upstream infrastructure. Reduced dry-season discharge is consistent with limited river-aquifer recharge documented in FAO (2011) and IWM reports.

8. MANDATORY SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MANDATORY SCIENTIFIC LIMITATIONS NOTICE:
These results are preliminary. Statistical tests are computed from satellite records available from January 2020 to July 2026 (primary analytical window; N observations drawn from that record). A robustness window spanning June 2018 to July 2026 uses the same GRACE-FO instrument only. Correlation does not imply causation. GRACE/GRACE-FO mascon solutions have coarse spatial resolution (~300 km footprint) and reflect total water storage changes across all vertical layers (surface water, soil moisture, and deeper aquifers combined). Findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution and validated against ground-based piezometric and hydrometric monitoring networks across Bangladesh.
