The Bengal Water Paradox

Witness Reports Contradict: Is Bangladesh Drying Out or Drowning?
Witness A: Inland Northwest
CRACKED SOIL & DROUGHT

Local well reports and studies cite dropping water tables, dry suction tubewells in Rajshahi, and desertification risks during the Boro season.

Witness B: Northeast & Coast
EXTREME DELUGE & FLOODS

Regional papers report intensifying monsoon cloudbursts, severe flash flooding in Sylhet haors, and coastal sea-level inundation.

★ The Challenge: Cut through the noise and verify true long-term trends using NASA Earth observations.
Core Hypothesis

Where is Bangladesh's Water Going?[cite: 1]

Do rainfall and water-storage trends differ across divisions, and are those differences statistically significant?[cite: 1]

The Spatial Granularity Rule

  • National averages hide regional catastrophe by cancelling out opposite dynamics.
  • Dividing analysis across all 8 Administrative Divisions balances physical precision with sensor limits[cite: 1].
  • Northwest vs Northeast divergence under the same monsoon system.

Statistical Engine

Filtering Noise from Climate Signal

Answering NASA's core challenge: proving statistical significance
STEP 1

STL Decomposition

Strips out the overwhelming annual monsoon waveform to uncover underlying secular trends.

STEP 2

Sen's Slope Estimator

Calculates robust, non-parametric physical rate of change (mm/yr for rain, cm/yr for water storage).

STEP 3

Mann-Kendall Test

Mathematically evaluates whether the trajectory is a real trend or a random anomaly (target: p < 0.05).

Verified: p < 0.05 Required for Policy Action
