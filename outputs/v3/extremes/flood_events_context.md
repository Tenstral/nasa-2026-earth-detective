## 2. Granular Flood Meteorological Synopses

### [2004] Catastrophic Multi-Basin Flood (Brahmaputra & Ganges)
- **Period & Inundation:** July–August 2004 | Inundation: ~38% (39 districts affected; population affected: —).
- **Hydrological Observations:**
  - National Monsoon (JJAS): 2020.9 mm (+21.3% vs baseline).
  - Northeast Region Monsoon: 2464.0 mm (+23.1%).
  - Northeast Pre-Monsoon (MAM): 931.8 mm (+27.3%).
  - Northeast June Deluge: 520.4 mm (-14.6%).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Concurrently intense Brahmaputra and Ganges monsoonal flood waves combined with sustained localized rainfall.
- **Source Authority:** [FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2004, Executive Summary, p. 1](http://www.ffwc.gov.bd/images/annual04.pdf)

### [2007] Prolonged Double-Peak Monsoon Flood
- **Period & Inundation:** July–September 2007 | Inundation: ~32% to 42% (40 districts affected; population affected: —).
- **Hydrological Observations:**
  - National Monsoon (JJAS): 1996.2 mm (+19.9% vs baseline).
  - Northeast Region Monsoon: 2472.0 mm (+23.5%).
  - Northeast Pre-Monsoon (MAM): 628.9 mm (-14.1%).
  - Northeast June Deluge: 789.2 mm (+29.5%).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Two successive peak flood waves in late July and early September driven by extreme Himalayan transboundary inflow.
- **Source Authority:** [FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2007, Executive Summary, p. 1–2](http://www.ffwc.gov.bd/images/annual07.pdf)

### [2017] Devastating Pre-Monsoon Flash Flood + August Monsoon Flood
- **Period & Inundation:** April (Haor) & August 2017 | Inundation: >70% Haor basins (April); ~42% country (August) (6 Haor districts (April); 31 districts (August) districts affected; population affected: —).
- **Hydrological Observations:**
  - National Monsoon (JJAS): 1878.6 mm (+12.8% vs baseline).
  - Northeast Region Monsoon: 2337.0 mm (+16.7%).
  - Northeast Pre-Monsoon (MAM): 1091.8 mm (+49.1%).
  - Northeast June Deluge: 668.7 mm (+9.8%).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Extreme April rainfall in Meghalaya/Cherrapunji (>1,000 mm in 10 days) submerging standing Boro crops in Sylhet/Sunamganj haors; followed by heavy August northern riverine floods.
- **Source Authority:** [FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2017, Executive Summary, p. 1–3](http://www.ffwc.gov.bd/images/annual17.pdf)

### [2020] Historical Long-Duration Flood (~40+ days)
- **Period & Inundation:** June–August 2020 | Inundation: ~30% to 33% (39 districts affected; population affected: —).
- **Hydrological Observations:**
  - National Monsoon (JJAS): 1809.2 mm (+8.6% vs baseline).
  - Northeast Region Monsoon: 2445.6 mm (+22.2%).
  - Northeast Pre-Monsoon (MAM): 761.8 mm (+4.0%).
  - Northeast June Deluge: 681.5 mm (+11.9%).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Record-sustained monsoonal precipitation across the upstream Brahmaputra basin (longest duration above danger level since 1998).
- **Source Authority:** [FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2020, Executive Summary, p. 1–3](http://www.ffwc.gov.bd/images/annual20.pdf)

### [2022] Centennial Record Flash Flood (Meghna/Surma-Kushiyara Basin)
- **Period & Inundation:** May–June 2022 | Inundation: Local: 94% Sunamganj, 84% Sylhet (~22–26% national) (18 districts affected; population affected: —).
- **Hydrological Observations:**
  - National Monsoon (JJAS): 1363.6 mm (-18.1% vs baseline).
  - Northeast Region Monsoon: 2002.9 mm (+0.1%).
  - Northeast Pre-Monsoon (MAM): 943.4 mm (+28.9%).
  - Northeast June Deluge: 978.6 mm (+60.6%).
- **Meteorological & Hydrological Drivers:** Unprecedented extreme rainfall in Meghalaya/Cherrapunji (>972 mm in 24h on 17 June) causing catastrophic rapid flash inundation across Surma-Kushiyara river system.
- **Source Authority:** [FFWC/BWDB Annual Flood Report 2022, Executive Summary / Flash Flood Chapter, p. 1–5](http://www.ffwc.gov.bd/images/annual22.pdf)

## 3. Scientific Synthesis: Why Floods Fail to Arrest Multi-Decadal Storage Depletion

1. **Surface Runoff vs. Subsurface Infiltration:**
   - In catastrophic flood years like **2004** (monsoon rainfall: 2,021 mm, +21.3% above baseline) and **2007** (1,996 mm, +19.9% above baseline), seasonal bulk storage rebounds reached 56.7 cm and 64.8 cm respectively.
   - However, the seasonal storage response ratio remained bounded between 28% and 33%, consistent with precipitation rates exceeding soil infiltration capacity, resulting in overland ponding, drainage congestion, and estuarine discharge into the Bay of Bengal rather than unbounded subsurface retention.

2. **Transient Crests vs. Multi-Year Secular Depletion:**
   - While catastrophic floods produce short-lived positive storage crests (+41.0 cm in 2007 and +33.1 cm in 2004), these anomalies dissipate within 3–4 months as monsoon waters drain into the delta.
   - By the subsequent pre-monsoon dry season (March–May), intensive agricultural abstraction for the dry-season Boro crop offsets the seasonal flood surplus, returning total water storage to its secular depletion trajectory.

3. **Localized Sylhet/Haor Deluges Decoupled from National Monsoon Aggregates:**
   - In **2017**, pre-monsoon rainfall in the Northeast reached **1,091.8 mm (+49.1% above normal)**, generating catastrophic flash flooding across the Haor basin in April before the monsoon onset.
   - In **2022**, while national monsoon rainfall was below average (1,363.6 mm, -18.1%), June rainfall in the Northeast reached **978.6 mm (+60.6% above baseline)**, submerging 94% of Sunamganj and 84% of Sylhet, showing that regional extremes can diverge sharply from national seasonal statistics.
